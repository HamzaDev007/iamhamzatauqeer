Work I’ve led

Client names stay private.

We rebuilt the same cloud stack 32 times

Technical lead, 12 to 14 engineers

The first version ran on a managed serverless framework built for a team of one or two. Then 12 to 14 engineers started deploying to it. Stacks locked, rollbacks failed, and about 32 times in a few months we tore everything down and rebuilt it. The team lost 24 to 30 hours a week to troubleshooting and regression testing.

I brought evidence: every failure, the hours it cost, and the framework’s known limits, checked with a former AWS engineer. I took it to the client’s principal solution architect, who had 16+ years of experience. He approved the change.

We moved the backend to roughly 80% containers and 20% serverless functions and left the frontend alone, because rebuilding both would have blown the MVP budget. The rebuilds stopped. Each brand’s data stayed isolated from the others, and the team could ship independently again.

A dashboard out of sync, and a client who began to doubt us

Team lead and delivery lead, on a smart-home platform with 6M+ connected devices

At the second meeting after kickoff, the client’s energy dashboard no longer matched reality. He started questioning whether we could deliver. The backend and data teams were each pointing at the other.

I was the lead, so it was mine to fix. I took it to my manager and director, then sat down with the data lead directly. I told him that if the project works we all succeed, and if it fails we all fail, and that backend would give him whatever support he needed. We delivered the milestone on time.

The same project moved a legacy backend from AWS to Azure, serving roughly 1M+ users, and was load-tested with 1M devices in batch.

Receipts read by AI, checked by a person

Delivered the MVP of an AI bookkeeping platform

Someone has to type every receipt into the books. We built a custom OCR model to read them and a review screen where a person confirms or corrects each field. Every correction goes back into training, so the model improves over time.

The person stays in the loop on purpose. I think AI should do the typing, and someone accountable should check the result.

How I think

  • Validate before you invest.Too many people spend serious money building an idea nobody checked.
  • Understand the problem before you build.A checklist of deliverables can be fully delivered while the original problem stays open.
  • Sometimes the right answer is to build nothing yet.Other times it’s improving what you already have, or automating one step.
  • Know what you’re paying for.You should hear why something is being built, what it costs, and what the trade-offs are, in plain language.
  • Keep a person accountable for AI.AI output needs checking in context before it drives a decision.
