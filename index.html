Technical lead, TkXel

We rebuilt the same cloud stack 32 times

Ended a cycle that cost a team of 12 to 14 about 24 to 30 hours a week.

From 32 rebuilds to none

  • Multi-tenant SaaS
  • AWS ECS
  • Lambda
  • EventBridge

The first version ran on a managed serverless framework built for a team of one or two. With 12 to 14 engineers deploying to it, stacks locked and rollbacks failed. About 32 times in a few months, we tore everything down and rebuilt.

I brought evidence: every failure, the hours lost, and the framework’s known limits, checked with a former AWS engineer. The client’s principal solution architect, who had 16+ years of experience, approved the change.

We moved the backend to roughly 80% containers and 20% serverless functions and left the frontend alone to protect the MVP budget. The rebuilds stopped, each brand’s data stayed isolated, and the team could ship independently again.

At the second meeting after kickoff, the client’s energy dashboard no longer matched reality, and he began to question whether we could deliver. Backend and data were each pointing at the other.

I was the lead, so it was mine to fix. I took it to my manager and director, then sat down with the data lead directly: if the project works we all succeed, and if it fails we all fail. I committed backend support to unblock his work, and we delivered the milestone on time.

The same project moved a legacy backend from AWS to Azure, serving roughly 1M+ users, and was load-tested with 1M devices in batch.

Human in the loop

  • OCR
  • Vue.js
  • NestJS
  • AWS

Someone has to type every receipt into the books. We built a custom OCR model to read them and a review screen where a person confirms or corrects each field. Every correction goes back into training, so the model improves over time.

The person stays in the loop on purpose. I think AI should do the typing, and someone accountable should check the result.
